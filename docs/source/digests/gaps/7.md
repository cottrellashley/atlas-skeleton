## 0. Verdict, sources, legend

**Is it a gap?** Yes, but it is narrower than CRITIC.md states. Three findings narrow it:

1. **The test capabilities already exist.** The references' fixture serves three conformance capabilities that cover every step-3 mechanism, with documented behaviour:
   - `conformance.costly@v1` (Metered read);
   - `conformance.change@v1` (action: `plan`, `approve`, `run` as an operation);
   - `conformance.slow@v1` (read, operation delivery).

   §9.8.4 lets the real gateway mount `conformance.*` "only where its environment is `local` or `conformance`" (09-8 L136). Nothing new needs inventing. **CRITIC's pair (`costly` + `slow`) is not enough.** `slow` is a read: no `Idempotency-Key` is sent (09-6 L35) and it has no effect, so R08's crash-after-commit test cannot run on it. Only `conformance.change@v1:run` (effect `external`, delivery `operation`) can carry R08 and R06's approval half (§2 below).
2. **Part of R09 is already built.** `gateway/ports/audit.go` L8-59 defines immutable `Fact`s of the kinds `admission`, `refusal`, `outcome` and `settlement`, correlated by `RequestID`, `Attempt` and `Operation`. `pipeline.go` L242-248 already makes a durable admission the condition of dispatch.
3. **The pipeline defers the rest on purpose.** `pipeline.go` L234-235 says: "(Idempotency claims and approval evidence come with the first capability whose effect is not read.)"

What stays MISSING in every source, so it must be designed and recorded:
- the receipt's format and the interaction that issues it;
- how a budget authority is declared;
- the fencing mechanism;
- the name of the uncertain state;
- the fact schemas;
- the acknowledgement point;
- the requested-versus-confirmed cancellation wire.

§3-§7 give one PROPOSED design for each. They respect every wire constraint in §1. §9 drafts the ASSUMPTIONS.md entries. This digest modifies nothing in the repository.

## 3. R06: confirmation and approval receipts

### 3.1 What the sources say

- Evidence (DR L78): "A caller-controlled assertion cannot establish who approved an execution."
- Decision (DR L80): "Distinguish authenticated caller context from approval evidence. Bind an approval or confirmation receipt from a trusted interaction to the principal, action, canonical request/plan, scope, approved maximum and expiry. Define what policy tightening does to outstanding plans. Treat approval as live policy".
- RV L64: "A receipt from a trusted interaction, bound to the principal, address, normalised request, plan digest, scope, maximum and expiry". RV L18 adds "The header is the client's claim only."
- CORE L196: binds "to the effective principal, address, normalized request, plan digest, scope, limits, expiry and relevant policy. A changed plan or increased approved maximum needs a new decision."
- CORE L46: "Where an agent is distinguishable from an interactive person, that distinction comes from trusted authentication context, not an `is_human` parameter supplied by the agent."
- Actions (02-4):
  - `approve` is "by someone other than the requester, where the policy says" (L10).
  - "A plan expires; running an expired plan plans again" (L43).
  - "An `irreversible` action is approved by someone other than its requester, always" (L45).
  - ATLAS-ACTION-002: "A `run` whose policy needs approval is refused with `approval_required` until approved by another principal" (L48).
  - ATLAS-ACTION-003: "Every finished run has a receipt in its profile's schema, linked from the audit record" (L49).
  - A plan is "stored, with an id" (L57).
- 03 L703: "A plan records the fingerprint it was made under."
- Acceptance (DR L82): "An agent cannot authorize a new plan by changing a header, copying another user's receipt, increasing the amount or changing the request after approval."
- MISSING: the receipt's format; the "trusted interaction"; what policy tightening does to outstanding plans (DR L80 asks for it to be defined).

### 3.2 PROPOSED design: two kinds of evidence, both held by the gateway, neither carried by the client

**Principle.** The client never carries a receipt. §9 has no header or member for one (C5-C7), and adding one changes the wire. Evidence is a record in the gateway's decision store (§6.1.5 L135: decision state lives in the store). A call is matched to that record by what it is bound to. The `Atlas-Confirm` header stays exactly as §9 defines it, a claim (RV L18), and grants nothing unless a matching record exists.

**(a) The confirmation receipt** (Metered calls over a confirmation threshold; `conformance.costly`). IN SCOPE.

1. **Quote.** The caller's confirmation threshold (a governance `limit` with `kind` `confirmation`, `amount` and `unit`; C25) is below the estimate. The estimate is the profile's `usage.cost.price` in `usage.cost.unit` for a fixed-price capability. If no valid confirmation matches, the gateway writes a **quote** and answers `confirmation_required` 428 with `estimate` `{amount, unit}`. The quote holds:
   - `id` (random);
   - `principal`, tenant and environment;
   - `address` and the `exact` profile;
   - `request_digest` (SHA-256 of the RFC 8785 form of the normalised request, as `pipeline.go` L215-217 computes);
   - `scope_digest` (the caller's data scope for this call);
   - `policy_revision`;
   - `estimate` (`amount`, `unit`);
   - `expires_at`.

   No limit is reserved and no key is claimed (CORE L177: evidence is checked before the claim).
2. **Confirm.** A later call by the same principal to the same address carries `Atlas-Confirm` ≥ the quote's `amount`. The gateway looks for an unexpired, unconsumed quote with the same principal, address, `request_digest`, `scope_digest` and `policy_revision`. It must also hold that the current estimate ≤ `Atlas-Confirm`. On a match, the gateway consumes the quote (single use) and the call proceeds to the claim and reservation. The **confirmation receipt** goes into the admission fact (§6). It is `{kind: "confirmation", quote: id, principal, address, request_digest, scope_digest, policy_revision, accepted_max: <Atlas-Confirm>, unit, interactive: <X-Atlas-Interactive>, expires_at}`.
3. **No match.** `Atlas-Confirm` without a matching quote, or below the estimate, gets `confirmation_required` again, with a fresh quote. The header alone never admits.
4. **What it proves, stated honestly.** A confirmation receipt proves that this principal was shown this estimate for this exact request and scope, and accepted it at most `accepted_max`. It does **not** prove that a human approved. `X-Atlas-Interactive` is client-controlled (C7) and an agent holds its person's token (06-2 L71). So the receipt records `interactive` as a claim, never as evidence (CORE L46).

**(b) The approval receipt** (actions with `NeedsApproval`; `conformance.change`). IN SCOPE as mechanism.

1. **Plan.** `plan` (read) stores a plan record. It holds:
   - `id`, which is the plan's `plan` member;
   - `principal`, the planner;
   - `address` (the action) and the `exact` profile (03 L703);
   - `request_digest` of the normalised `plan` request;
   - `plan_digest` (SHA-256 of the RFC 8785 form of the plan result);
   - `scope_digest`, `policy_revision` and `expires_at` (02-4 L43).

   `plan` changes nothing (ATLAS-ACTION-001).
2. **Approve.** `approve` `{plan}` is a write, so it needs `Idempotency-Key` (C5) and an exact-address grant (C21). The caller must be a **different principal** from the planner (02-4 L10, L48). It writes an **approval receipt** `{kind: "approval", plan: id, plan_digest, planner, approver, policy_revision, expires_at: plan.expires_at}` and answers `{plan, approved: true}`. Approval by the planner is refused. **AMBIGUOUS:** with which code?
   - (a) `denied`, since a rule refuses it.
   - (b) `approval_required`, since it still awaits another principal.
   - Proposed: (b), because it tells the caller what to do next. Both are registry codes (C1).
3. **Run.** `run` `{plan}` requires a key. The caller must be the planner; another principal's plan is `not_found`, as ATLAS-MECH-007 does for operations (06-7 L52). The plan must be unexpired. **AMBIGUOUS:** an expired plan "plans again" (02-4 L43).
   - (a) The run refuses with `approval_required` and a `next` that says to plan again.
   - (b) The run re-plans implicitly, which needs a new approval anyway.
   - Proposed: (a).

   The run also needs an approval receipt whose `plan_digest` matches the stored plan, where the current environment is in `meaning.policy.approval`. Otherwise it gets `approval_required` 403. Grants, denies, data scope, protective limits and budgets are **rechecked at run** against current policy (CORE L30 "Execution always rechecks"; L176 "After current access checks").
4. **Policy tightening of outstanding plans** (DR L80 asks for this to be defined). Proposed:
   - (i) Approval required in more environments: approvals already given stand, per "runs already approved stand" (03 L699).
   - (ii) A revoked or denied grant, a narrowed scope or an exhausted budget refuses the run at the recheck.

## 4. R07: budget authority and atomic reservations

### 4.1 What the sources say

- DR L86: "Cosmos batch atomicity is limited to one container and logical partition, so the described layout does not atomically cover those shared pools."
- DR L88: "Separate protective rate limits from hard spending admission. Choose a transaction boundary per shared budget authority, co-locate its atomic reservation set or allocate bounded sub-budgets. Reject unsupported combinations initially. Prove any broader cross-budget scheme before promising exactness."
- CORE L224: "For the first implementation, use a declared budget authority per metered provider; reject unsupported combinations of independently partitioned hard budgets until an atomic design is proved. Do not label approximate per-replica limits exact."
- CORE L194: "An estimate says what was estimated, its unit, basis and uncertainty. Unknown cost remains unknown. ... A price or runtime estimate is not a hard cap unless the execution mechanism can enforce it."
- Spec mechanics this replaces: "A call **reserves** its upper bound before it starts, acts, then **settles** its actual use" (06-2 L129). Limits are "allowances (per caller), pools (shared, firm-wide) and ceilings (hard maxima), inherited down from the firm to a domain to a capability to an interaction, and an override may only tighten" (06-2 L129).
- Already in code: protective limits are per replica and "are not a budget (R07)" (`pipeline/limits.go` L10-13). `ports.Limit` has only `PerMinute`, `Concurrency` and `MaxRows` (`ports/policy.go` L56-68).
- Acceptance (DR L90): "Different users on different replicas contend for one shared hard budget; combined admissions never exceed it. Abandoned reservations recover, and settlement is idempotent." ATLAS-ACCESS-003: "a burst of twenty parallel calls starts at most what the budget allows" (06-2 L55).
- MISSING: how an authority is declared; where "refused" happens; the reservation-recovery bound.

### 4.2 PROPOSED design

1. **Declaration.**
   - A metered provider declares **one budget authority id** when it registers. It is a field of the registry entry, next to `Provider` (`core/registry/registry.go` L54-62), for example `conformance` for `conformance.costly`.
   - A capability with trait `Metered` must name an authority, and its `usage.cost.unit` is that authority's unit. A `Metered` capability without one fails at registration (as ATLAS-MECH-017 fails a bad module at start, 06-7 L62).
2. **Budgets are governance, not contract** (C24). Add `Budgets []Budget` to `ports.Policy`, beside `Limits`. Each `Budget` is `{ID, Authority, Subject ("*" = a pool shared by every caller, or a principal, group or role), Capabilities, Unit, Amount (decimal string), Period}`. Governance's `limit` serves them with `kind` `budget`, `amount`, `unit`, `period`, `set_by`, `resets` and `remaining` (C25). The confirmation threshold is the same: `kind` `confirmation`, `amount`, `unit`.
3. **Unsupported combinations are refused at policy load, not at call time.** When a policy revision loads, a budget whose capabilities span more than one authority, or whose unit differs from its authority's unit, makes the whole revision **refused**. The previous revision keeps serving, and the failure is reported in readiness and the audit. This mirrors the snapshot rule: "A snapshot that fails is refused, the failure is reported in readiness and the audit, and the previous one keeps serving" (06-7 L218).
   - AMBIGUOUS: the other reading refuses at the call, with `budget_exceeded` or `unavailable`. Load-time refusal fits "Reject unsupported combinations initially" (DR L88).
4. **One atomic reservation set per call, inside one authority.**
   - New port `Budgets` (decision state, §6.1.5 L135): `Reserve(ctx, authority, reservationID, draws []Draw{BudgetID, Period, Amount}, expiresAt) error`. It is all-or-nothing across every budget of that authority that applies to the caller and capability: allowance, pool and ceiling.
   - `Settle(ctx, authority, reservationID, actual Amount) error`. It is idempotent by `reservationID`; a second settle is a no-op.
   - `Release(ctx, authority, reservationID) error`.
   - Co-location: every counter of one authority is in one transaction boundary. For a future Cosmos adapter, that is one logical partition per authority. DEFERRED with Cosmos (RV L33). Bounded sub-budgets (DR L88) are DEFERRED.
5. **Amount reserved.** The upper bound is the profile's fixed `usage.cost.price`.
   - AMBIGUOUS / DEFERRED: a capability whose cost is unknown before execution. CORE L194 says "Unknown cost remains unknown", so no exact upper bound exists. Reading: a hard budget cannot be applied to it, and the policy load refuses that combination too. No journey or test capability has an unknown price.
6. **Refusal.** Reserving past a budget gives `budget_exceeded` 403, not retryable (C2).
   - A `Metered` call whose authority has no `Budgets` store configured gives `unavailable`, by analogy with "`unavailable`, `detail` naming Cosmos DB; an exact limit cannot be reserved" (06-1 L265).
7. **Abandoned reservations recover.** A reservation carries `expiresAt` = the call's execution deadline (§7) plus a grace period. `Reserve` first releases expired reservations of the same authority, in the same transaction. That needs no sweeper ("no sweeper; ... time-to-live on everything that expires", 06-1 L147).
   - A late `Settle` for a recovered reservation still charges `actual`. The money was spent, so the period can end over budget, and the settlement fact records it. CORE L194: an estimate "is not a hard cap unless the execution mechanism can enforce it".
8. **Order** (C17, C19). The idempotency lookup comes first, so a replay reserves nothing. Then the evidence check (§3), then the claim, then `Reserve`, then the admission fact. If the admission cannot be recorded: `Release`, unclaim, and `unavailable` (extends `pipeline.go` L245-248, which already releases the protective limit).
9. **`Atlas-Limit-Remaining`** is a Dictionary of **Integers** (C26), and budgets are decimals (`12.500`). AMBIGUOUS:
   - (a) omit budgets from the header and serve them exactly through governance `remaining` and `atlas.usage`;
   - (b) send the integer floor in the budget's unit.

   Proposed: (a). An integer cannot carry `87.5` exactly, and the header lists `rate` and `concurrency` as its examples.
10. **Parameters (MISSING):** the reservation grace period (proposed 5 minutes, the `settlement` controller's resync, 06-1 L245), and how a `period` aligns. AMBIGUOUS: `P1D` from 00:00 UTC, or rolling? Proposed: UTC-aligned, with `resets` reported (C25).

## 5. R08: idempotency for the whole retention, fencing, the uncertain outcome

### 5.1 What the sources say

- Evidence (DR L94): takeover "releases the idempotency key so a start can run again. [§6.7] promises the same key returns the same operation. The upstream could already have committed when the receipt was lost."
- Decision (DR L96): "Retain one logical operation per key throughout its retention, including uncertain outcomes. Use provider deduplication and reconciliation. Fence workers after lease expiry. Include metered reads when a retry can duplicate a charge. Do not promise exactly-once external effects solely from HTTP deduplication."
- RV L66: "One logical operation per key for its whole retention, uncertain outcomes included. Workers are fenced after their lease and reconciled".
- CORE L198: "Idempotency keys identify one logical execution within tenant, principal, environment and capability-interaction scope. They bind to the normalized semantic request and execution-affecting options, not JSON whitespace or a mutable HTTP route."
- CORE L200: "Persist both the submitted request identity and its effective defaults at admission. A retry of the same submitted request uses the original normalization snapshot ... Transport-only changes do not create a new business execution. Current authorization can still refuse access to the retained result."
- CORE L202: "An uncertain upstream outcome remains recorded and is reconciled before retrying. A timeout or worker crash must not discard the key and authorize a second change. Exactly-once effects are promised only where the upstream mechanism supports them; otherwise the capability describes its reconciliation behavior."
- CORE L220: "do not erase an upstream system's native run identity."
- Lease values that stay: "a running operation carries its replica's lease, 30 s, renewed every 10 s, with its progress checkpointed in its record" (06-7 L184; 06-1 L250). Takeover "within one minute" (ATLAS-OPERATION-001, 06-1 L65). Resume "from its last checkpoint, at most three times" (06-1 L250).
- §6.7.8 values that stay: missing key on a non-read is `invalid_request`, target `Idempotency-Key` (L197). Same key with another request is `idempotency_conflict` (409) (L200). Same key while the first still runs is `in_progress` (409, retryable, `retry_after`) (L201). "Replay: The first answer, stored up to 1 MiB; an operation's start answers the same operation" (L202).
- Library defects to fix on import (patterns digest §3.1):
  - D1: a panic leaves the record `InProgress` for 24 h.
  - D3: the `keys` map is never cleared; after a sweep, `ErrNotFound` forever.
  - D5: the start key is not fingerprinted, so another request silently gets the first operation.
  - INTEGRATIONS L44: "retain uncertain effects instead of dropping keys on 5xx or response overflow". L45: "Persist key-to-operation ownership, use bounded durable workers and leases/fencing, reconcile on restart".
- Acceptance (DR L98): "Kill the worker after upstream commit but before receipt persistence; retry the original key. It returns/reconciles the original operation and creates no second effect."
- MISSING: the fencing mechanism; the name of the uncertain state.

### 5.2 PROPOSED design

1. **Idempotency record** (decision state, port `Idempotency`; 06-1 L135). Keyed by `(tenant, principal, environment, address, key)` (CORE L198). The environment is constant per deployment. AMBIGUOUS: the tenant. The build serves one tenant, so the tenant is recorded but not yet multi-valued. The record holds:
   - `fingerprint`;
   - `normalised_request` (the snapshot with defaults filled, CORE L200);
   - `state` ∈ {`claimed`, `completed`, `uncertain`};
   - `operation` (its name, for operation deliveries);
   - `answer` (≤ 1 MiB, for single deliveries);
   - `origin_request_id`;
   - `attempts`;
   - `expires_at`.
2. **Fingerprint.** SHA-256 of the RFC 8785 form of `{request: <the request resolved forward to the current profile, before defaults are filled>, execution: <Atlas-Execution or null>}`. Transport-only options are excluded: `Accept`, `Atlas-Deadline`, `Atlas-Confirm`, `traceparent`. AMBIGUOUS:
   - (a) JCS of the request as submitted (06-7 L199);
   - (b) after defaults are filled (06-7 L123);
   - (c) this proposal.

   Proposed: (c). Without defaults, a later change of default ("latest" date) does not conflict a retry. Forward resolution means a client that re-described between retries does not conflict. Either way, the replay uses the stored `normalised_request` (CORE L200). `Atlas-Confirm` must be excluded, or C12's repeat "with ... the same `Idempotency-Key`" would conflict.
3. **Lookup** (CORE step 5, after access checks). This is the order of `pipeline.go` steps 1-4, then new.
   - Same fingerprint, `completed`: replay the stored answer, or answer the same operation (202 with its `Operation-Location`). Record a `replay` fact. Reserve nothing.
   - Same fingerprint, `claimed` or `uncertain`, single delivery: `in_progress` 409 with `retry_after`. Operation delivery: answer the same operation.
   - Other fingerprint: `idempotency_conflict` 409.
   - Current authorization may still refuse (CORE L200).
4. **Claim** (CORE step 6). The claim is an atomic create-if-absent, after the evidence check (§3) and before `Reserve` (§4). A failed claim race goes back to the lookup.
5. **Retention.**
   - The key never expires while its operation is `pending` or `running`, including while reconciling.
   - It is kept ≥ 24 h after the operation ends (C10; 06-7 L191, L203; CORE L212).
   - For a single delivery: 24 h after the answer was stored.
   - The `release` of ATLAS-OPERATION-002 is never implemented (OVERRIDDEN BY REVIEW: R08).
6. **Fencing (fencing token).**
   - The operation record carries `lease {holder, epoch, until}` and a store `version`.
   - Acquiring or taking over the lease is a conditional write on `version` that increments `epoch`.
   - Every write a worker makes is conditional on `lease.epoch == mine`: progress, checkpoint, outcome. That uses the store contract's "every write is conditional on the store's own version" (06-7 L255).
   - A worker whose lease lapsed (clock ≥ `until`) or whose epoch is stale gets a refusal from the store. It stops, and it writes nothing.
   - Every upstream call carries the call's key or the operation's name (C23), so a fenced zombie's late upstream call is de-duplicated there.
   - Values: lease 30 s, renewed every 10 s (06-7 L184). Time comes from `kit/clock` (C27).
7. **Takeover = reconcile, never release.** The `operations` controller (06-1 L244; leader-elected, 06-7 L172) finds a lapsed lease, takes it (epoch+1) and asks the provider to `Reconcile(operation name or key)`. The answer is one of:
   - `committed` + receipt: the operation becomes `succeeded` with that receipt as `result`.
   - `not_started`, and the work is resumable: resume from the checkpoint, at most 3 times (06-1 L250).
   - `not_started`, and not resumable: `failed` with problem `unavailable`. The key stays bound to this failed operation.
   - `unknown`: stays `running` (5.2 item 8). Reconciliation is retried with the controller backoff, "50 ms doubling to 5 minutes" (06-7 L167). It is alerted after an hour ("keys failing for over an hour", 06-7 L174).
   - A provider without `Reconcile` cannot serve a non-read operation (its registration fails). That is "the capability describes its reconciliation behavior" (CORE L202).
   - AMBIGUOUS: may a key whose provider confirms `not_started` be released?
     - (a) No: one key, one logical operation, for its retention (RV L66).
     - (b) Yes: nothing happened, so nothing would be a "second change" (CORE L202).
     - Proposed: (a), the stricter and simpler. The caller retries with a new key. The `failed` problem `unavailable` is `retryable: true` (C2), but a client retry with the same key returns the same failed operation. That is consistent with ATLAS-PROTOCOL-005 (04 L116).
8. **The uncertain state.** MISSING in every source. CORE L212: "Unknown outcomes stay pending reconciliation." The six states are fixed (C8). Proposed: **no new state**. The operation stays `running`, which is non-terminal, so clients keep polling (C10) and the key stays held. The audit history shows it as `unresolved` (§6).
   - AMBIGUOUS: should the operation document also show it? Readings:
     - (a) nothing more;
     - (b) an additive optional member, such as `"reconciling": true`. It is legal because the operation schema is open (C9) and readers ignore unknown members (CORE-TOL-001). But it changes the independent references (D22, 08 L44), so the product owner decides.
     - (c) a new state value. This is a protocol change; clients would read it as `UNKNOWN`.
   - Proposed: (a) now, (b) when an owner asks for it.
   - For a single-delivery write with an uncertain outcome, the answer is `in_progress` (409, retryable), never success.
9. **Metered reads** (DR L96, CORE L198). §9 requires a key only on non-reads (C5), and CORE-CONFIRM-001 relies on no key being sent on `conformance.costly` (C14). Proposed:
   - The gateway **accepts and honours** an `Idempotency-Key` on a `Metered` read when one is sent (same record, same rules), but does not require it.
   - Without a key, each call is a new execution.
   - AMBIGUOUS: should clients send one? §9.7.4 step 7 sends a key only for non-reads (C11). Changing that is a client protocol change: DEFERRED, for the product owner.
10. **Answers over 1 MiB** (patterns A4). §6.7.8 stores "up to 1 MiB"; R08 forbids dropping the key. No in-scope single-delivery write has a large answer: `approve` returns `{plan, approved}`. Proposed: any larger result must use operation delivery (`result_location`, C10). A single-delivery write whose answer exceeds 1 MiB is a profile lint failure. DEFERRED until a capability needs it.

## 6. R09: admission, outcome and settlement facts, and the acknowledgement point

### 6.1 What the sources say

- DR L102: §6.2.8 "requires final usage and source history in one immutable record durable before the response ends"; settlement and streaming come later.

- C18: every attempt leaves a fact.
- Acceptance (DR L106): "Crash at each boundary. The history preserves what is known, correlates later settlement and exposes an unresolved outcome rather than inventing a completed one."
- Content to keep (06-2 L146): the address, the caller, the client and edge, each hop; the decision and its reason; the writer and expected fingerprints; the governance revision; the limits reserved and settled; the execution, rows, bytes and duration; the source's own record, copied in at settlement; the data version; "the outcome and, for actions, the receipt". The request is "a hash of its canonical form ... results are never recorded" (06-2 L148).
- Already built (`ports/audit.go`):

  - Kinds `admission`, `refusal`, `outcome`, `settlement` (L48-53).
  - "An admission that cannot be recorded is not dispatched" (L55-56).
  - The file sink fsyncs each line before `Record` returns (`auditlog.go` L1-5, L70-72).
- Gaps in today's code:
  - Refusals are recorded best-effort: "a refusal is refused whether or not it is recorded" (`pipeline.go` L150).
  - Outcome errors are discarded: `_ = p.opt.Audit.Record(...)` (`pipeline.go` L267).
  - The edge never sets `Attempt`, so it is always 1 (`edge.go` L299; `pipeline.go` L146).
  - There is no `replay` kind and no fact id.
- MISSING: the fact schemas; the acknowledgement point; the meaning of "attempt id".

### 6.2 PROPOSED design

1. **Fact kinds.** `refusal`, `admission`, `replay` (new; C18 "including a replay"), `outcome`, `settlement`.
   - An outcome written by reconciliation carries `Operation` and `ReconciledFrom` = the epoch that took over.
   - Add fields:
     - `FactID` = `request_id` + kind + sequence, for de-duplication on re-emit. Log Analytics reads de-duplicate by `request_id` (06-2 L153), which would collapse facts, so the reader must use `FactID`.
     - `Evidence` (the R06 receipt of §3.2) on the admission;
     - `Reservation` (id, authority, draws) on the admission;
     - `Settled` (reservation id, actual, unit) on the settlement;
     - `Receipt` (the action's result `{plan, changed}`) on the outcome (06-2 L146 "for actions, the receipt"; ATLAS-ACTION-003).
   - No token, key, body or query string (MECH-014, AGENTS.md L47).
   - AMBIGUOUS: may the `Idempotency-Key` itself be recorded? "No ... key ... in any log, span, metric or audit record" (AGENTS.md L47) reads as covering it. Proposed: record its SHA-256 only.
2. **Correlation.**
   - `request_id` = `X-Request-Id`, one per HTTP request, so one per attempt.
   - `attempt` = the idempotency record's attempt counter for keyed calls, else 1.
   - `origin_request_id` = the first attempt's id, which joins the history.
   - `operation` = the operation name.

   This settles "attempt id" (CORE L226). AMBIGUOUS: no header carries a client attempt number, so the gateway counts attempts per key.
3. **The acknowledgement point** (MISSING; DR L104 asks for it).

   | Event | What must be durable before it | If recording fails |
   |---|---|---|
   | Dispatch to a provider | The `admission` fact, `AuditSink.Record` returned nil (fsynced), after the claim and the reservation | `Release` the reservation, remove the claim, answer `unavailable` 503 with `retry_after` (extends `pipeline.go` L245-248) |
   | `202` for an operation | The idempotency claim, the operation record (`pending`) and the `admission` fact. **This is the point at which Atlas owns the work** | As above; nothing dispatched |
   | A refusal answer | The `refusal` fact is attempted first | Still refuse (refusing is fail-safe); mark the audit sink unhealthy in readiness. AMBIGUOUS: ATLAS-ACCESS-004 wants it "durable before the call's answer ends"; today's code allows a refusal to go unrecorded (`pipeline.go` L150) |
   | Success of an operation | The operation record's terminal state, written under the lease epoch. This is the system of record for the outcome | The `outcome` fact is appended after; if that fails, the `operations` controller re-emits it from the record on resync until it lands (idempotent by `FactID`). **This is the recovery when an effect succeeds but recording completion fails** (DR L104) |
   | End of a synchronous answer (single, or chunked after the end line) | Nothing more: the answer was already sent | The `outcome` fact is attempted. If it is missing (crash, failed write), the joined view shows `unresolved`; nothing is invented |
   | Settlement | `Budgets.Settle` (idempotent) | The `settlement` fact is appended; a retry re-appends by `FactID` |

4. **The joined summary = `audit_record`** (C9: open; required `request_id`, `time`, `address`, `caller`, `decision`, `outcome`; builtins L959-966). One row per origin request:
   - `decision` from the refusal or admission;
   - `limits` from the reservation and settlement;
   - `outcome` from the latest outcome fact.
   - With no outcome fact: `outcome: {}` (its members are optional, builtins L1136-1149) plus the additive member `"resolution": "unresolved"`. With one: `"resolution": "resolved"`.

## 7. R17: separate deadlines, requested versus confirmed cancellation

### 7.1 What the sources say

- DR L144: "Separate start-request deadline, execution deadline and operation retention. Report requested versus confirmed cancellation; preserve uncertain committed effects." Evidence: §4.1 "couples disconnect to cancellation".
- CORE L210: "An HTTP disconnect during startup does not mean a successfully admitted durable operation was cancelled. Clients can recover the operation using the same idempotency key."
- CORE L212: "Cancellation means a request to stop. The operation reaches `cancelled` only when the provider knows work stopped; an already committed effect is reported honestly. Unknown outcomes stay pending reconciliation. Retention must cover the active operation and the promised retry/recovery window, even when work lasts more than 24 hours."
- Spec values that stay:
  - Operation deadline: "The interaction's `deadline.max`; past it the work is cancelled and the operation `failed` with `deadline_exceeded`" (06-7 L186).
  - Cancel is "cooperative: forwarded to every hop" (06-7 L187); "acknowledged at once" (04 L198).
  - `pending → cancelled` and `running → cancelled` are both allowed (04 L88, L91).
  - Retention: "24 hours after it ends" (06-7 L191).
  - Synchronous calls: `Atlas-Deadline` capped at `deadline.max` (`pipeline.go` L199-208).
  - "A caller that went away is logged as 499 and answered nothing" (06-7 L130).
- MISSING: the wire for "requested versus confirmed".

### 7.2 PROPOSED design

1. **Three clocks.**

   | Clock | For a synchronous call (single, chunked) | For an operation delivery |
   |---|---|---|
   | Start-request deadline | = `Atlas-Deadline`, capped at `deadline.max` (today's `pipeline.go` L199-208) | = `Atlas-Deadline` of the start POST. Bounds only admission, the claim, the record and the 202 |
   | Execution deadline | = the start-request deadline | = operation `created` + the interaction's `deadline.max` (06-7 L186): `PT10M` for `conformance.slow` and `conformance.change:run` (FIX). Past it, cancel the work; `failed` with `deadline_exceeded`, **unless** the outcome is uncertain, in which case it stays `running` and is reconciled (§5.2 item 8) |
   | Retention | Idempotency record: 24 h after the answer is stored | Operation and key: all of its active life, plus ≥ 24 h after it ends (C10; CORE L212) |

   AMBIGUOUS: can an operation's execution deadline be set by the caller? Readings: (a) always `deadline.max`, as 06-7 L186 states; (b) a caller option. No header exists for it. Proposed: (a).
2. **Disconnect.**
   - A synchronous read whose caller disconnects is cancelled: the context ends and the source query stops. The answer is logged as 499 (06-7 L130); no effect exists to preserve.
   - For an operation start, the work runs under a context detached from the HTTP request (`context.WithoutCancel`, through `kit/conc.Go`; C27). A disconnect after the 202's preconditions are durable (§6.2 item 3) does not cancel it. The client recovers with the same key (CORE L210).
   - AMBIGUOUS: a **read** with operation delivery (`conformance.slow`) carries no key (C5). A client that loses the 202 cannot recover the handle, and the orphan operation runs to its end and expires. Proposed: accept that for reads (no effect), and honour a key on such reads when one is sent (§5.2 item 9).
3. **Requested versus confirmed.** Proposed: with no wire change, the cancel's own answer reports it.
   - `POST <Operation-Location>:cancel` durably records `cancel_requested_at` on the operation record, forwards the cancel to the provider (by operation name or tag, 06-7 L187), and waits for confirmation, bounded by one poll interval (1 s for `conformance.slow`, README L180). This is modelled on `subscribe`'s bounded wait for `ready` (06-1 L248). It then answers the operation:
     - `state: "cancelled"`: **confirmed**; the provider knows the work stopped. A `pending` operation that never started is confirmed at once (04 L88).
     - `state: "running"` (or `"pending"`): **requested** and not yet confirmed. The later polls show the outcome.
     - `state: "succeeded"`: the effect had already committed, "reported honestly" (CORE L212), with its `result`/receipt.
     - Stays `running` while uncertain: reconciliation as in §5.2 item 7.
   - AMBIGUOUS: should a later `get` show "cancel requested"? Readings: (a) not shown; (b) an additive optional member, e.g. `cancel_requested` (date-time), legal under C9 but a references change (D22); (c) a new state, a protocol change. Proposed: (a) now, (b) for the product owner.
   - The library's defect D4 must not be carried over: "any error with client-cancel cause ends Canceled". Only the provider's confirmation ends `cancelled`. Map `context.Canceled` explicitly (patterns digest §2.2, L135).
   - CORE-OP-001 still passes (C15): `conformance.slow`'s fake stops at once, so the cancel answers `cancelled`; the following `get` shows `cancelled`; one `POST …:cancel`.
