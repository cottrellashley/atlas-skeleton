## Verdict

**It is a real gap, and it is decided below.** RV:30 (step 2) asks to "split the contract fingerprint, the document revision and the routing (R01, R02)", RV:59-60 states the outcome, and no source says *how*. The open points are listed in §1.

**The decision in one paragraph.**
- §9.4 stays unchanged, bindings included.
- The client caches a contract by its exact-id, forever.
- The client routes a call from the profile its environment's gateway most recently named as current for that `id@vN`. It learns that profile from describe or from `Atlas-Profile`, and fetches the document by `exact`.
- A lock pins the reader contract only.
- The gateway answers every describe (and every other built-in and interaction) `Cache-Control: no-store`, with no ETag.
- It keeps every binding of every profile of a live version routed.
- The client-side catalogue cache is deferred.
- A guide's `revisions` carries a digest of the whole served profile document.

This reading passes every fingerprint vector and the scenarios CORE-LOCK-001, CORE-DISC-002, CORE-BOOT-001, CORE-DISC-001, CORE-CALL-003, CORE-RES-001, CORE-RES-003, CORE-TOL-001 and CORE-GUIDE-001 (traces in §4). It also meets both review acceptance tests (§5).

The critic's proposed reading (CRITIC.md:62-66) is adopted. Four additions close points it left open:
- D5: old bindings stay routed;
- D4.3: the "newest" profile is a per-environment pointer;
- D6: the catalogue cache is deferred;
- D7: the guide's document revision.

### 1.7 The open points the decision must settle

1. §9.4:44 and §3:333 make the fingerprint a strong ETag, but case `prose-changed-and-reordered` gives one fingerprint to two documents. R01 (DR:34) needs a validator that changes with the representation.
2. §9.6:95 makes describe by exact `immutable`, but `describe.schema.json` (L7-12) requires `profile`, `governance`, `availability` and `eligibility`, three of which are live. R01 says they are "private and uncached".
3. §9.7:103 caches the catalogue for one hour, while §9.6:95 says everything but bootstrap and describe is `no-store` (DR:36).
4. §9.4:28 keeps bindings in the contract, while R02 (RV:60) says "Routing is fetched as current metadata". Nothing says how a locked client learns a newer binding (DR:42).
5. §9.7:88's "the newest cached profile of that version" has no ordering and no environment.

## 3. The decision (D1-D9)

Each item says who builds it. The critic's step numbers (CRITIC.md:53-56) are: step 6 the built-ins `atlas.describe` and `atlas.catalog`; step 8 the edge's `Cache-Control` and `ETag`; steps 9-10 the Go client, engine, CLI and Node. RV's own "order of work" has steps 1-5 (RV:27-33), and this whole decision is RV step 2. The step 4-11 work order is not reproduced here, so I take the critic's numbering as given.

### D1. The contract fingerprint stays §9.4 exactly (IN SCOPE, no code change)

- The projection, the algorithm and the 16-hex fingerprint are unchanged (§9.4:15-37). Bindings stay in it.
- Why:
  - every vector's canonical form includes the bindings (§1.2);
  - the fixture's `#d0a0…` differs from `#f173…` only by a binding address (§1.2);
  - §3:593-595 already classes a binding change as Safe, which §3:571 defines as "A new fingerprint";
  - R02 asks to "refresh current routing independently" (DR:44), which D4 does without touching the projection.
- The host part of routing is already current metadata: a binding names a bootstrap `server`, whose `url` comes from the bootstrap (§9.6:15). The client revalidates the bootstrap at every connect (CORE-BOOT-001). Moving a gateway host changes the bootstrap, not any fingerprint.
- Rejected: removing `bindings` (or `deadline`) from the projection. It breaks AP-FP-001 and every exact-id in the fixtures, and RV:21 and RV:70 forbid that.

### D2. The three revisions, as Atlas fields (IN SCOPE)

| AC:156-160 revision | In the first build | Where it is sent | Cache rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Contract fingerprint | `profile.fingerprint` (§9.4); the `exact-id` `id@vN#fp` | `Atlas-Profile`, `Atlas-Profile-Expect`, the lock, the describe answer's `profile.fingerprint`, catalogue items' `exact` | The client keeps it forever by exact-id (D4.1) |
| Document revision | Two values: (a) `profile.revision`, the owner's integer, served unchanged (§3:145; `profile.schema.json` L289-292); (b) the **document digest**: the first 16 lowercase hex characters of SHA-256 over the RFC 8785 form of the whole profile document as served (prose included). The gateway computes (b), so it "Changes when the served content changes" (AC:159) with no owner discipline | (b) appears only in the guide's `revisions` (D7). Neither appears as an HTTP ETag (D3) | Never used as a cache key in the first build (nothing is HTTP-cached) |
| Policy and runtime | `governance.governance_revision` and `for_caller`; `availability`; `eligibility`; house rules' `revision`; the bootstrap's `catalog_etag` | The describe answer; `Atlas-Governance-Revision` (§9.6:42); catalogue pages | **Never cached as authority.** The gateway decides every call afresh (AC:30, AC:160) |

Registry rule (follows from §3:108 and §9.4:58): the gateway may register a new *document* for an existing exact-id, with the same projection and different prose. It then serves the newest document for that exact-id, both by `exact` and by `id`. Two documents with different projections under one fingerprint are refused (§9.4:58).

| `atlas.guide`, `atlas.catalog`, `atlas.search`, every other built-in and interaction answer | `no-store` | None | §9.6:95 "Everything else is `no-store`"; §9.6:50 |

- `If-None-Match` sent on a POST to describe or catalog is ignored; the edge never answers `304` to a POST. Every interaction is `POST` (§9.6:20), and the spec gives no POST revalidation.
- §2.6:178's "revalidates with `If-None-Match` and gets `304`" for the catalogue and profiles is therefore DEFERRED (A6). The client still parses `Cache-Control` values (the `headers.json` vectors include `max-age=31536000, immutable`, AP-HTTP-001), but no gateway answer uses them.

### D4. How every client caches and routes (IN SCOPE, steps 9-10; Go, and Node, which is written independently)

**D4.1 The contract cache.**
- Profiles are kept by exact-id, forever, in memory and at `<ATLAS_HOME>/cache/profiles/<exact-id>.json`.
- The file holds the profile document exactly as served, written atomically (temp file, then rename, as §9.7:78 does for tokens), mode `0600`, directory `0700`.
- The layout is the one the live Go and Node clients already share (`sdk/go/discover.go`:146,169; `clients/node/src/index.ts`:299,312).
- Every profile is checked by fingerprint whenever it is received (§9.7:104) and whenever it is loaded from disk. A mismatch from the gateway is `invalid_document` (CORE-DISC-001). A mismatch from disk discards the file and fetches the profile again **by `exact`**.
- From a cached profile, the client uses only its contract: schemas, interactions and bindings, traits, error codes, meaning, evolution. Its prose may be older than the gateway's (D2 registry rule) and is never shown as current. People read prose through `describe` or `guide`, which always ask the gateway.

**D4.2 Describe and guide operations.**
- The client operations `describe` and `guide` (§9.7:23-24) always call the gateway. They are never answered from a cache, because the answer is live caller state (R01).
- The profile inside a describe answer is admitted into the D4.1 cache after its fingerprint is checked.

**D4.3 The current-profile pointer, which defines §9.7:88's "newest cached profile".**
- Per environment, the client keeps a map from a **pinned id** (`id@vN`) to the exact-id the gateway most recently named as current for it.
- It is stored at `<ATLAS_HOME>/cache/current-<env>.json`, a JSON object `{"<pinned-id>": "<exact-id>"}`, written atomically, mode `0600`. `<env>` is the bootstrap's `environment`, as for the token file (QUESTIONS.md:9, Q-CLI-1).
- It is set:
  - (a) from `profile` when a describe by `id` answers with a pinned id;
  - (b) whenever an interaction answer's `Atlas-Profile` names an exact-id of the same `id@vN`. The client then fetches that profile by `exact` if it is not cached (§9.7:95).
- Last seen wins. §3:851 says the gateway writes under its current profile, so the most recent `Atlas-Profile` is the current one.
- A bare id (no `@vN`, §9.7:58) is resolved by describe `by: "id"` once per process and **never** persisted, so "the current stable version" can move.
- If the pointer names an exact-id that is neither cached nor fetchable (`not_found` by `exact`), the pointer entry is dropped, and the call continues by D4.4 without it.

**D4.4 The call, by profile role.** This refines §9.7:88-96.

| Role | Which profile | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| **Reader** | Locked: the lock entry's exact-id (§9.7:88, :144). Unlocked and pinned: the pointer's exact-id; if there is none, describe `{"by": "id", "id": "<pinned-id>"}` **once**, which sets the pointer | Request validation (step 5); `Atlas-Profile-Expect` (step 7); the target of result resolution (step 10); the interaction's `takes` and `returns` schema names |
| **Routing** | The pointer's exact-id for that `id@vN` if set, else the reader | Binding choice (step 6, §4:281), the server URL from the bootstrap (§9.6:15), the interaction's `delivery`, `execution` and `deadline` (`default`, `max`) for step 7; critical traits (step 3), checked on the routing **and** the reader profile |
| **Writer** | The `Atlas-Profile` of the answer (step 9) | Decoding the answer (step 10), and the chain to the reader (§9.5:17, by `exact`) |

- Why this is safe:
  - within a version, the gateway accepts a request written under any earlier profile (§3:583 `BACKWARD_TRANSITIVE`; ATLAS-RESOLVE-003, §3:841);
  - every binding of one interaction serves the same result (ATLAS-BINDING-002, §4:265);
  - removing an interaction is Breaking (§3:604), so the routing profile always has the reader's interaction.
- A locked program **never** calls `atlas.catalog`, `atlas.search` or `atlas.describe` `by: "id"` for a locked capability. It may call `atlas.describe` `by: "exact"`. This is "metadata resolution" (AC:164), and it is exactly what CORE-LOCK-001 allows.
- **OVERRIDDEN BY REVIEW: R02** over §9.7:88 (bindings from the locked profile), `00-vision.md`:64, :71 and D39 (`08`:61).
- A client never uses `governance`, `availability` or `eligibility` from a describe answer to allow, skip or refuse a call. Only the gateway refuses (§9.7:105; AC:30). This is what makes R01's "no cached access response authorizes the call" (DR:38) hold.

### D5. The gateway routes every binding of every live profile (IN SCOPE, the registry and edge, step 8)

- The front door's route table maps **every** `https` binding address of **every** profile the gateway serves for a live `id@vN` (the whole chain, not only the current profile) to that interaction.
- The request is resolved from its `Atlas-Profile-Expect`, never from which binding it came in on.
- This is §4:262's "the old binding stays served for a migration window", with the window set to **until the version is retired**. It matches §3:107: a profile is served "for as long as anything may hold it (at least until its version's sunset)".
- Registration refuses a binding address that any live profile of a *different* capability or interaction holds (§4:261).
- Effect: a client with a stale pointer, or a locked client, still reaches the interaction. Its first answer's `Atlas-Profile` moves the pointer (D4.3b), so later calls take the new route.
- In the first build every binding is the gateway's own (`/v1/<family>/<id>@v<N>:<interaction>`, §4:283), and moving a provider inside the one deployable (RV:13) changes no binding at all (§4:250).

### D6. The catalogue: no client-side catalogue cache in the first build (DEFERRED). OVERRIDDEN BY REVIEW: R01 (DR:36) over §9.7:103 and §2.6:178

- Every `catalog` and `search` operation calls the gateway. Their answers are `no-store` (D3).
- `connect` reads only the bootstrap. It makes no catalogue call (CORE-DISC-001 count 4; CORE-LOCK-001 count 0).
- This reconciles the one-hour cache with `no-store` by not caching. No scenario asserts a catalogue cache hit.
- AP-CLIENT-004 (§9.7:197) and §9.7:105 hold, because an unseen pinned id is always described.
- When the cache is built (later): it is keyed by environment and principal, lives at most 1 hour, and is dropped when the bootstrap's `catalog_etag` (required in `bootstrap.schema.json` L14, L76-79; §9.3:17) differs from the one it was filled under. That is the "revalidated with its ETag at every `connect`" of §9.7:103, with no POST revalidation. It is only for listing and completion, never for authorisation and never to refuse a call.

### D7. Guide identity (IN SCOPE, step 6). OVERRIDDEN BY REVIEW: R01 ("A guide's identity includes the prose revision", RV:59)

- `atlas.guide` is generated on every call from the current snapshot, and answered `no-store`. Clients never cache a guide.
- The `revisions` object (`builtins.schema.json` `guide_result`, L369: "The revisions it was written from: the profile's prose, governance and context (Q-BI-2)"; its members are not fixed, QUESTIONS.md:77) carries:
  - `contract`: the fingerprint;
  - `document`: the D2(b) digest of the whole served profile document;
  - `governance`: the governance revision;
  - `context`: the managed-context revision, when there is one.
- §2.6:152's determinism then reads: the same `document`, `governance`, `context` and client give the same guide.
- CORE-GUIDE-001 scripts the guide answer, so these member names are not tested there. They are this decision's (A8).

### D8. The describe answer (IN SCOPE, step 6)

- The shape is unchanged: `{profile, governance, availability, eligibility}` (`describe.schema.json` L7-12; §9.3:22, :52).
- The request is `{"by": "id", "id"}` (a capability-id or pinned-id) or `{"by": "exact", "exact"}` (`builtins.schema.json` `describe_request`).
- By `exact` for an older profile of a live version, it answers that profile's newest registered document, with the **current** governance, availability and eligibility. Eligibility is computed over that profile's bindings.
- By `id`, it answers the current profile.
- The answer's `Atlas-Profile` is the built-in's own exact-id (§9.3:45). The fixture has `atlas.describe@v1#ff036976dde88885`.

### D9. The lock (IN SCOPE as written; packaging DEFERRED)

- The lock pins reader contracts only: `version` and `fingerprint` (`lock.schema.json`). It never pins routing, permissions, source data or availability (AC:162; DR:44).
- "Package reader contracts with the lock" (DR:44) and "includes the contracts needed to start" (AC:162) are DEFERRED. The lock's format is left to owners (RV:99). The first build fetches lock entries by `exact` (CORE-LOCK-001 L4: "fetched by exact").
- A no-metadata-request deployment bundle with "an explicitly supported stable dispatch route" (AC:164) is DEFERRED.

## 7. Ambiguities left, each with its readings and the choice made

- **A1. Bootstrap `Cache-Control`.** §9.6:95 says only "by `ETag`".
  - Readings: (a) `no-cache`; (b) `max-age=N` plus ETag; (c) none.
  - Chosen: (a). It makes every connect revalidate, as CORE-BOOT-001 shows.
- **A2. The profile's "HTTP ETag" (§9.4:44, §3:333).**
  - Readings: (a) send `ETag: "<fp>"` on describe; (b) send none.
  - Chosen: (b). A strong validator must change with the representation (DR:34), and prose can change under one fingerprint (vector case 1). Describe is `no-store`, so no validator is needed.
- **A3. "Newest cached profile of that version" (§9.7:88).**
  - Readings: (a) the head of the `evolution.previous_profile` chain among cached files; (b) the last profile named current by this environment's gateway; (c) the latest fetched across all environments.
  - Chosen: (b). (a) fails for unlinked variants: the fixture's `#d0a0…` has no `previous_profile`. (c) would send a dv profile to pd.
- **A4. Which profile governs `delivery`, `execution` and `deadline` when reader ≠ routing.**
  - Chosen: routing (D4.4), as current metadata. The alternative is the reader. The gateway enforces its own deadline maximum either way.
- **A5. The migration window (§4:262).**
  - Readings: (a) a fixed period; (b) until the version is retired.
  - Chosen: (b) for the first build (D5).
- **A6. Catalogue and profile `304` revalidation (§2.6:178) over POST.**
  - Chosen: none in the first build. The bootstrap's `catalog_etag` is the revalidation channel. DEFERRED.
- **A7. Prose updates under one exact-id.**
  - Readings: (a) the registry keeps the first document; (b) it serves the newest.
  - Chosen: (b) (D2 registry rule). Clients may hold older prose; only the contract is used from the cache.
- **A8. The `revisions` member names in `guide_result` (Q-BI-2: "Its members are not fixed").**
  - Chosen: `contract`, `document`, `governance`, `context`. Pass 1 used `prose`.
- **A9. ATLAS-DISCOVERY-007's 60 s / 15 min.**
  - With events deferred (RV:15) and no catalogue cache, a client learns a new profile on its next answer's `Atlas-Profile`. An idle client learns nothing until then.
  - Readings: (a) this meets "reaches" for routing purposes; (b) a 15-minute bootstrap or catalogue poll is needed.
  - Chosen: (a) for the first build; (b) DEFERRED with D6.
- **A10. Cache isolation by tenant.** The handbook wants caches "isolated by that effective context" (`review/CAPABILITY-HANDBOOK.md`:33). Tenant switching is left to owners (RV:98).
  - Chosen: profiles are shared by exact-id (a global contract identity), and the pointer is per environment. Per-tenant isolation is DEFERRED.
- **A11. The pointer and contract file layout** is not in the spec. Go and Node must agree on it, because they may share `ATLAS_HOME`. D4.1 and D4.3 fix it, and it must be recorded (§8).
